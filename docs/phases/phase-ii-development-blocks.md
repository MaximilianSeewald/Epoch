# Phase II: Development blocks and player acceptance

**Status:** binding content sequencing and design acceptance criteria. This is not a technical implementation plan or feasibility assessment.

## Block A — Awakening (Chapters 1–3)

**Story scope:** individually delayed return about five in-game years after Phase I completion; permanent closure respected; scar response, class-specific opening, first Nullwarden encounter, broken-code investigation. Preserve Phase I choice continuity and mystery pacing.

**Gameplay scope:** present the Phase I permanent contamination consequence as a durable Planet Feature while preserving its exact intended gameplay meaning and class variant; introduce Resonance Protocol and Stability; first incidents, player-selected approach, real naval threat and science/defense alternatives, wreck or signal analysis, first three technology leads.

**In-game result:** players recognize the old scar as an evolving physical feature, win/avoid the first threat at tangible cost, and obtain a counter-pattern plus an early technology research path. No empire completes the campaign solely through elapsed time.

**Content dependencies:** Phase I conclusion and opt-out outcome; saved contamination class/severity and prior decisions; Phase I scar effect; chapter outcomes that can record win, retreat, loss or refusal.

**Player acceptance:** after the individual delay the continuing empire receives one clear start; a closed empire receives none; the feature shows a lasting physical scar and retains the prior penalty; organics/hives/machines receive distinct framing; the first fleet can cause meaningful loss; a weak research empire has a costly plausible alternative; a lost ship/wreck does not strand the chapter.

## Block B — Expansion (Chapters 4–6)

**Story scope:** second infection-like expression, ambiguous multi-system pattern, laboratory consequences, returning and adapted Nullwardens. The network feels geographically real and the hunters react to prior tactics.

**Gameplay scope:** all four feature states and transitions; state-based incidents; Resonance Laboratory with risk/upkeep; actual science-ship expeditions to multiple systems; alternate archive routes; second real naval encounter with adaptations; technologies and wreck analysis influencing defense.

**In-game result:** the empire maps a convergence location and has a documented containment/exploitation posture. At least two worlds or systems may be involved through explicit action; no automatic silent spread. Combat, defensive, research and withdrawal routes remain consequential.

**Content dependencies:** Block A counter-pattern, recorded battle/analysis outcomes, technology research availability and stability; at least one missing-object fallback per expedition/encounter.

**Player acceptance:** planetary consequences remain visible and meaningful; every incident has a meaningful action; the lab is useful but not mandatory; multiple actual systems are explored; prior choices change the details; the second encounter responds to player strategy; losing access produces a lower-yield path rather than a lockout.

## Block C — Resonance (Chapters 7–8)

**Story scope:** multi-stage chamber operation, consequential countermark disclosure, four distinct local resolutions and a satisfying Phase II ending that points toward Phase III without explaining EPOCH's ultimate purpose.

**Gameplay scope:** explicit costs and actions for chamber foundations, stability-sensitive risks and recovery, resume/downscale/seal options, durable ending consequence, closure of the situation and recorded Phase III clue. New technologies have campaign and general use.

**In-game result:** the local crisis reaches a player-chosen resolution; the scar remains materially present with its Phase I cost carried forward; ending choices change world/network/strategic conditions. The empire is ready to discover a larger question later, not compelled to pursue a prescribed ideology.

**Content dependencies:** two node findings or defined fallback, battle/withdrawal outcome, prior feature and stability state, minimum research leads. Every path into Block C has a chamber observation or sealing option.

**Player acceptance:** all four endings are achievable with distinct costs and advantages; low stability remains recoverable or resolvable at a cost; failure leaves a scar but not a stuck campaign; the ending closes Phase II; the final clue confirms connection without revealing authorship or purpose.

## Overall scope acceptance

Phase II feels 1.5–2 times Phase I through eight chapters, meaningful research and military decisions, real multi-system work, state-specific incidents and durable consequences. Remove artificial calendar waits as a substitute for player activity. Every chapter requires a choice, investigation, project, encounter resolution or explicit refusal. The campaign is individually owned, class-aware, and consistent with Phase I closure and outcome records. Block acceptance is based on what the player can see and do; technical feasibility and implementation verification are outside this design specification.
