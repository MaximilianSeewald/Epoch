# Phase II: Development blocks and player acceptance

**Status:** binding content sequencing and design acceptance criteria. This is not a technical implementation plan or feasibility assessment.

## Block A — Awakening (Chapters 1–3)

**Story scope:** the wait begins with Phase I's final decision; continuing empires return after approximately five in-game years with a few months' variation, while permanent closure receives no re-entry. Possession or loss of either the original contamination world or capital does not rewrite the empire's campaign record. The contamination site responds; the capital's permanent conclusion scar remains a distinct class-specific opening detail. Include the first Nullwarden encounter and broken-code investigation.

**Gameplay scope:** represent both Phase I records: the original contamination world's actual result and the permanent capital conclusion scar with its full negative economic effect. Preserve both as durable Planet Feature outcomes and combine them only if their saved locations coincide. Include remote/archive continuity for unavailable worlds. Introduce Resonance Protocol and Stability; first incidents, real naval threat, science/defense alternatives, wreck or signal analysis, and precise research gates for the first three technologies.

**In-game result:** players recognize the old scar as an evolving physical feature, win/avoid the first threat at tangible cost, and obtain a counter-pattern plus an early technology research path. No empire completes the campaign solely through elapsed time.

**Content dependencies:** Phase I conclusion and opt-out outcome; saved contamination class/severity and prior decisions; Phase I scar effect; chapter outcomes that can record win, retreat, loss or refusal.

**Player acceptance:** the wait starts at the final Phase I choice; continuing empires begin near five years later with small variation and closed empires receive none; loss/transfer of either planet does not erase or move its consequence; both features retain their separate Phase I results and effects unless collocated; classes receive distinct framing; a weak research empire has a costly plausible alternative; a lost ship/wreck/site does not strand the chapter.

## Block B — Expansion (Chapters 4–6)

**Story scope:** second infection-like expression, ambiguous multi-system pattern, laboratory consequences, returning and adapted Nullwardens. The silent system contains the chapter's emotional/scientific centerpiece: precise observation of a suppressed signal and a player choice that alters the evidence and exposure, without resolving who made it or why.

**Gameplay scope:** all four contamination-site states and transitions while the capital conclusion scar remains; state-based incidents; Resonance Laboratory; actual multi-system expeditions with archive routes; second real naval encounter; Adaptive Resonance Armor and Phased Sensors available early enough to research before that encounter.

**In-game result:** the empire maps a convergence location and has a documented containment/exploitation posture. At least two worlds or systems may be involved through explicit action; no automatic silent spread. Combat, defensive, research and withdrawal routes remain consequential.

**Content dependencies:** Block A counter-pattern, recorded battle/analysis outcomes, technology research availability and stability; at least one missing-object fallback per expedition/encounter.

**Player acceptance:** both Phase I locations and effects remain visible/accounted for; silent-system choice changes evidence/exposure; every incident offers meaningful action; multiple systems are explored; prior choices alter details; technologies are normally researched, not granted; the second encounter responds to player strategy; losing access produces a lower-yield path rather than a lockout.

## Block C — Resonance (Chapters 7–8)

**Story scope:** multi-stage chamber operation, consequential countermark disclosure, four distinct local resolutions and a satisfying Phase II ending that points toward Phase III without explaining EPOCH's ultimate purpose.

**Gameplay scope:** Resonance Field Control becomes a normal research option during chamber preparation and can aid the following operation. Provide a costly/no-tech alternative. Include explicit chamber actions, stability-sensitive recovery, four durable endings with distinct economic/military/risk outcomes, and differentiated Phase III information positions while preserving both original Phase I planet consequences.

**In-game result:** the local crisis reaches a player-chosen resolution; the scar remains materially present with its Phase I cost carried forward; ending choices change world/network/strategic conditions. The empire is ready to discover a larger question later, not compelled to pursue a prescribed ideology.

**Content dependencies:** two node findings or defined fallback, battle/withdrawal outcome, prior feature and stability state, minimum research leads. Every path into Block C has a chamber observation or sealing option.

**Player acceptance:** all four endings are achievable without a technology deadlock and have distinct planetary, economic, military, risk and information consequences; low stability remains recoverable or resolvable at a cost; lost expedition members are not returned in dialogue; failure leaves consequences but not a stuck campaign; Phase II closes without revealing authorship or purpose.

## Overall scope acceptance

Phase II feels 1.5–2 times Phase I through eight chapters, meaningful research and military decisions, real multi-system work, state-specific incidents and durable consequences. Remove artificial calendar waits as a substitute for player activity. Every chapter requires a choice, investigation, project, encounter resolution or explicit refusal. The campaign is individually owned, class-aware, and consistent with Phase I closure and outcome records. Block acceptance is based on what the player can see and do; technical feasibility and implementation verification are outside this design specification.
