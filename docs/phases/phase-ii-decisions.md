# Phase II: Decision tree and consequence matrix

**Status:** binding choice ledger for the Phase II campaign. Outcomes are empire-local and feed later scenes; none is an automatic best strategy.

## Decision flow

```text
Phase I conclusion = continue
  └─ ~5 years from that empire's conclusion → Chapter 1 (scar awakens)
       ├─ observe / research perimeter / suppress → survey and baseline
       └─ Chapter 2: fight / defend & divert / withdraw and recover
            └─ Chapter 3: restrained analysis / rapid reverse-engineering / remote substitute
                 └─ Chapter 4: quarantine / study / exploit
                      └─ Chapter 5: listening array / mobile expedition / paired survey
                           └─ Chapter 6: engage / trap / countermeasure / withdraw
                                └─ Chapter 7: isolate / synchronize / redirect; activate or seal
                                     └─ Chapter 8: boundary / guarded listening / turn outward / break chamber
```

Phase I permanent closure branches out before the delayed entry and has no active return. During Phase II, each chapter records success, costly success, loss/retreat or conscious refusal. Refusal closes or changes the current investigation through an in-world action; it never advances the player without consent to a materially new stage.

## Cross-phase carry-through

| Phase I record | Phase II effect |
|---|---|
| First Echo approach | Changes Chapter 1 data quality and one Chapter 3 interpretation; cautious route reveals a safeguard, aggressive route an earlier tactical clue. |
| Missing Expedition recovery/loss/abandonment | Surviving personnel/records can provide a human or technical perspective. Recorded loss is respected; archive substitutes exist. Expedition loss increases caution/cost in one experiment but grants no arbitrary punishment. |
| Contamination class and severity | Sets the original Planet Feature expression, persistent baseline effect and incident framing. Severe neglect makes instability more likely and raises containment cost, while granting a stronger research clue. |
| Silent Structure secured/investigated/destroyed | Secured yields a safer node access clue; studied yields richer but uncertain data; destroyed yields a damaged echo and chamber safety disadvantage. Each still leads to Chapter 5. |
| Conclusion assessment and permanent consequence | Sets the initial scar severity and informs Chapter 8's closing interpretation. The permanent negative gameplay effect is preserved through all feature state changes. |
| Continue versus permanent closure | Continue enables the individually delayed campaign. Closure blocks active Phase II and later re-entry, preserving existing consequences. |

## Phase II decision consequences

| Decision point | Immediate trade-off | Later observable consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Chapter 1 posture | Observation sacrifices speed; perimeter costs; suppression trades research for stability. | Changes initial stability, baseline quality and first Nullwarden clue; no gate is removed. |
| Chapter 2 response | Combat risks ships; defense/diversion costs time/resources; withdrawal loses access and yields less evidence. | Changes wreck quality, scar damage and Chapter 3/6 tactical information. All can progress. |
| Chapter 3 analysis | Restraint is slower; rapid work risks instability; remote study costs more and yields less. | Changes tech timing, clue reliability and Chapter 4 incident severity. |
| Chapter 4 protocol | Quarantine limits yield; study accepts controlled exposure; exploitation creates highest instability. | Alters secondary feature possibility, stability, lab benefits and chamber risk. |
| Chapter 5 expedition plan | Arrays incur upkeep; mobile study risks ship time; paired survey limits coverage but improves corroboration. | Determines node detail, available chamber safeguards and one Phase III lead. |
| Chapter 6 response | Engagement risks fleet; trap needs preparation; countermeasure needs research; withdrawal loses a node. | Changes technology evidence, chamber component cost, network condition and stability. |
| Chapter 7 foundation | Isolation is safe and limited; synchronization maximizes knowledge and risk; redirection favors defense but exposes the trace. | Sets ending availability emphasis and permanent cost; all permit a Chapter 8 resolution. |
| Chapter 8 resolution | See ending table in campaign specification. | Creates one durable world/network outcome and one distinct Phase III lead. |

## Ending consequence matrix

| Ending | Planet and economy | Strategic/research outcome | Phase III handoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keep the boundary | Controlled scar; original Phase I penalty retained; continuing monitoring cost. | Low incident burden; modest research loss versus active listening. | A stable but incomplete countermark. |
| Listen under guard | Controlled/Active bounded scar; upkeep and recurring risk remain. | Strongest continuing study benefit and lab utility. | A maintained channel with a new unexplained reply. |
| Turn signal outward | Scar retains its class and prior cost; threat shifts away, but instability/exposure obligation rises. | Strong defensive warning or fleet advantage, ongoing strategic attention. | The network has noticed the empire's countermark. |
| Break the instrument | Chamber collapsed; permanent local damage or transformed scar, with baseline Phase I effect retained. | Removes chamber incidents and yields less ongoing research. | A countermark fragment survives in records or wreckage. |
