# Phase II: Decision tree and consequence matrix

**Status:** binding choice ledger for the Phase II campaign. Outcomes are empire-local and feed later scenes; none is an automatic best strategy.

## Decision flow

```text
Phase I conclusion = continue
  └─ ~5 in-game years from that empire's final Phase I choice (small variation) → Chapter 1 (contamination site responds)
       ├─ observe / research perimeter / suppress → survey and baseline
       └─ Chapter 2: fight / defend & divert / withdraw and recover
            └─ Chapter 3: restrained analysis / rapid reverse-engineering / remote substitute
                 └─ Chapter 4: quarantine / study / exploit
                      └─ Chapter 5: listening array / mobile expedition / paired survey
                           └─ Chapter 6: engage / trap / countermeasure / withdraw
                                └─ Chapter 7: isolate / synchronize / redirect; activate or seal
                                     └─ Chapter 8: boundary / guarded listening / turn outward / break chamber
```

The wait starts at the moment of Phase I's final decision, whether it is to continue or permanently close the investigation. Continuing empires begin after approximately five in-game years, with a variation of several months. During this interval, old consequences persist and only subtle non-progressing symptoms may appear. There is no active campaign invitation or chapter start. Permanent closure branches out before delayed entry and has no active return. Ownership change, loss or destruction of either recorded planet does not reset the wait, transfer the campaign to a new empire, or erase the old empire's record. During Phase II, each chapter records success, costly success, loss/retreat or conscious refusal. Refusal closes or changes the current investigation through an in-world action; it never advances the player without consent to a materially new stage.

## Cross-phase carry-through

| Phase I record | Phase II effect |
|---|---|
| First Echo approach | Changes Chapter 1 data quality and one Chapter 3 interpretation; cautious route reveals a safeguard, aggressive route an earlier tactical clue. |
| Missing Expedition recovery/loss/abandonment | Surviving personnel/records can provide a human or technical perspective. Recorded loss is respected; archive substitutes exist. Expedition loss increases caution/cost in one experiment but grants no arbitrary punishment. |
| Original contamination location, class, severity and actual Phase I result | Sets the contamination-site Planet Feature/state, its saved effect and incident framing. Severe neglect makes instability more likely and raises containment cost, while granting a stronger research clue. If inaccessible, use its recorded evidence without claiming the site is currently owned. |
| Silent Structure secured/investigated/destroyed | Secured yields a safer node access clue; studied yields richer but uncertain data; destroyed yields a damaged echo and chamber safety disadvantage. Each still leads to Chapter 5. |
| Capital conclusion scar and permanent consequence | Sets the separate capital Planet Feature and preserves its complete permanent negative effect through Phase II. It is not treated as the contamination site unless their recorded locations match. |
| Final decision time | Starts the individual ~five-year wait for a continuing empire, with slight variation. Permanent closure remains ineligible; ownership changes do not reset the original empire's elapsed interval or campaign record. |
| Continue versus permanent closure | Continue enables the individually delayed campaign. Closure blocks active Phase II and later re-entry, preserving existing consequences. |

## Phase II decision consequences

| Decision point | Immediate trade-off | Later observable consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Chapter 1 posture | Observation sacrifices speed; perimeter costs; suppression trades research for stability. | Changes initial stability, baseline quality and first Nullwarden clue; no gate is removed. |
| Chapter 2 response | Combat risks ships; defense/diversion costs time/resources; withdrawal loses access and yields less evidence. | Changes wreck quality, scar damage and Chapter 3/6 tactical information. All can progress. |
| Chapter 3 analysis | Restraint is slower; rapid work risks instability; remote study costs more and yields less. | Changes tech timing, clue reliability and Chapter 4 incident severity. |
| Chapter 4 protocol | Quarantine limits yield; study accepts controlled exposure; exploitation creates highest instability. | Alters secondary feature possibility, stability, lab benefits and chamber risk. |
| Chapter 5 expedition plan | Arrays incur upkeep; mobile study risks ship time; paired survey limits coverage but improves corroboration. | Determines node detail, available chamber safeguards and one Phase III lead. |
| Chapter 6 response | Engagement risks fleet; trap needs preparation; countermeasure needs research; withdrawal loses a node. | Changes technology evidence, chamber component cost, network condition and stability. |
| Chapter 7 foundation | Isolation is safe and limited; synchronization maximizes knowledge and risk; redirection favors defense but exposes the trace. | Sets ending availability emphasis and permanent cost; all permit a Chapter 8 resolution. |
| Chapter 8 resolution | See ending table below and the campaign specification. | Preserves both Phase I records and creates a distinct planet/economy/warfare profile and Phase III evidence posture. |

## Ending consequence matrix

| Ending | Planet and economy | Strategic/research outcome | Phase III handoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keep the boundary | Contamination site becomes Bound Resonance; capital conclusion scar and its full penalty remain separately. Site receives low incident burden and a permanent monitoring imprint. | Ongoing monitoring/upkeep; lowest research yield and no military bonus; safer local productivity. Residual resonance risk is low but returns if monitoring lapses. | Reliable suppression map and a stable but incomplete countermark; safest and least informative starting position. |
| Listen under guard | Contamination site remains Controlled-with-channel; capital scar remains unchanged. Both records persist. | Continuing research and sensor-warning benefit; recurring incidents and upkeep reduce economic efficiency. A live channel can intensify later. | Richest local measurements and an active unexplained reply; Phase III begins with the channel open. |
| Turn signal outward | Contamination site is Redirected/Active; capital scar remains unchanged. The source is scarred by the redirection and less productive. | Defensive warning and modest military readiness benefit; increased instability, periodic incidents and network exposure. | Outbound bearing and tactical warning; best prepared militarily but already visible to the network. |
| Break the instrument | Chamber destroyed; accessible contamination site gets permanent Fracture/Ruin and local output/research loss; if unavailable, archive evidence is damaged instead. Capital scar keeps its complete Phase I penalty. | Ends chamber upkeep and military exposure; no continuing research/fleet bonus. Rare weak echoes remain a risk. | Damaged countermark fragment, no live channel and the safest immediate posture; least precise map. |
