# EPOCH Phase II: Resonance

**Status:** Binding design specification for content review. Design only; no Phase II gameplay is implemented by this document.
**Campaign owner:** each continuing empire independently. **Duration target:** 1.5–2 times Phase I in meaningful play, not calendar delay.

Phase II continues the individual empire's Phase I record. Its wait begins when the player makes Phase I's final decision: either to continue or to close the investigation. A continuing empire receives the new opening about five in-game years later, with a small variation of several months so empires do not all begin on the same date. The wait is not measured from game start. A permanent closure choice bars active re-entry. Both the original contamination outcome and the permanent Phase I conclusion scar remain accounted for during the wait. Subtle, non-progressing symptoms may be noticed, but there is no Phase II situation, project, active dispatch or chapter start before the wait ends.

Phase I left two related but distinct records. The **contamination site** is the world where First Contamination occurred, with its actual Phase I result (contained, worsened, spread, or otherwise resolved) and any feature or lasting site consequence. The **conclusion scar** is the permanent, class-specific negative effect placed on the capital at the Phase I conclusion. Those worlds can coincide, but the design treats them separately unless their saved locations are the same. Phase II's Planet Feature presentation accounts for both without removing or silently relocating either consequence. The contamination site's changing state anchors the mystery; the capital scar records the lasting cost of the empire's first contact. Something is answering the old signal through the contamination record. The Nullwardens appear to be suppressing that answer, but their attacks make it louder. The empire learns that the contamination site, the capital scar, distant anomalies, and hostile machines share a pattern; it does not learn who made the pattern or its ultimate purpose. Phase II resolves the immediate crisis while leaving the larger mystery open for Phase III.

## Continuity contract

At entry, preserve the Phase I approach, expedition outcome, contamination type/severity/site/result, Silent Structure resolution, conclusion assessment, capital scar, permanent negative effect, and final continuation/closure decision. An empire that closed Phase I receives no invitation, situation, project, or active dispatch. If either recorded world changed hands but still exists, its feature and planet-bound effect remain on that world; the campaign-owning empire retains its history and does not regain the world or duplicate its feature elsewhere. If a world was destroyed or is no longer accessible, preserve its historical outcome and consequences in the empire's record; the campaign follows available archives, surviving site evidence, or a remote investigation. The contamination site's feature reflects what actually happened there. The capital's conclusion feature reflects the permanent conclusion scar and keeps its economic consequences. If both are on one world, both records are expressed together as distinct aspects of that world's feature; if not, each remains on its own world. An unavailable world's outcome appears in later scenes without creating a feature on an unrelated planet or rewriting the record. Neither consequence is simply removed: suppression changes the contamination site's state and visible expression; destruction or sacrifice transforms the affected local outcome into another lasting consequence.

Each chapter has a recorded outcome: success, costly success, retreat/loss, or conscious refusal. No chapter closes by elapsed time alone. A player can resume after setback, choose a fallback, or end the investigation in-world. Failure and loss change rewards and later options; they never strand the campaign in a state with no actionable route.

## Chapter 1 — The Scar Wakes

About five years after the empire made its Phase I final decision, instruments at the recorded contamination site register a reply in the abandoned signal band. If that world is inaccessible or lost, surviving instruments, expedition records or the capital's scar provide the lead, and the opening investigation occurs through an accessible archive/site. The reply is not a voice: coastlines, root networks, and buried infrastructure produce synchronized pulses. Survey crews report that familiar damage has acquired geometry. The old contamination is reacting to something beyond the system. The separate capital scar remains visible and costly but need not itself be resonating.

**Play:** an opening narrative event and a sequence of local planetary incidents establish Active Resonance. The player selects one of three initial postures: observe at a distance (slower, safest, better baseline data); open a limited research perimeter (moderate cost, unlocks the lab path); or impose emergency suppression (stability gain now, reduced early research and a later trust penalty from the phenomenon). A science ship performs a local survey project and records a baseline. Planet incidents ask the player to protect residents/worker processes, preserve samples, or prioritize infrastructure; the choices affect stability and the scar's severity, not whether the campaign starts.

**Class expression:** organics experience synchronized dreams, mineral blooms and altered ecological rhythms; hive minds feel a foreign cadence entering peripheral drones, with a choice to isolate or listen through expendable nodes; machines observe recurring clock drift and memory-address rearrangement, and choose between sandboxing, emulation or resource isolation. None is immune or treated as a biological infection.

**Completion:** baseline report received and an approach chosen. If the original site or a survey ship is lost or unavailable, a staffed observatory at an accessible owned site or remote archive study is the costly fallback. Refusal closes the active investigation with both the recorded contamination outcome and capital scar/Phase I malus intact and a durable “sealed records” outcome.

## Chapter 2 — The First Hunters

A compact unknown fleet enters the contamination system, if accessible, and targets the recorded resonance source, not the capital or civilian traffic. If the site is lost or outside the empire's reach, it targets an accessible evidence cache or listening post instead. It attacks after a brief warning burst made of the same broken intervals found in the Phase I records. The empire cannot know whether the attackers are rescuers, custodians or executioners.

**Play:** the Nullwardens are an actual hostile fleet and can damage ships and the contamination site or listening post. The player can engage, evacuate vulnerable assets and establish a defensive cordon, or attempt a science-led diversion while defenses hold. A weak research empire can use a prepared fortified starbase, allied assistance where available, emergency evacuation and a risky signal-lure project; this route remains demanding and incurs a concrete cost, but does not demand a fleet it cannot plausibly field. A failed defense records damage/loss and unlocks debris recovery or an alternate signal study. No battle win is required to continue.

**Completion:** survive or resolve the threat through combat, diversion, or a recorded withdrawal-and-recovery path; then secure a sensor trace, wreck, or captured transmission. Losing or lacking the contamination world does not erase its outcome, feature where it remains, or any consequence already applied; the campaign proceeds through an accessible archive, recovered sample, or later expedition, with a more costly and less controlled chapter path. The capital conclusion scar remains separate throughout.

## Chapter 3 — The Broken Code

The trace is not a language but a correction sequence. Nullwarden weapon harmonics attempt to cancel the scar's pulse, yet each cancellation creates a small echo elsewhere. Researchers discover that the hunters respond to deviations in the pattern rather than to political borders.

**Play:** analyze a wreck, clean signal or fallback archive; compare it with the Phase I record; choose forensic restraint, rapid reverse-engineering, or destructive disassembly. Research projects establish the first campaign technology opportunities. Rapid work produces earlier military insight at the cost of lower stability and a corrupted clue. A lost wreck is replaceable with a longer multi-system listening project, but yields less component data.

**Completion:** one analysis route yields a confirmed “counter-pattern” and a strategic lead to other systems. This ends Block A and opens Block B. A failed experiment causes an incident and instability, then permits a contained repeat or remote analysis. The empire knows the attackers are reacting to a signal, not why.

## Chapter 4 — The Second Infection

The scar's cadence reappears in a second form: nearby organisms, machines or geological processes begin echoing fragments without sharing the original contamination. The player must decide where the boundary of the original wound ends.

**Play:** select a quarantine perimeter, controlled exposure study, or resonance exploitation trial. Planetary incidents are matched to Latent, Active, Unstable or Controlled state. Choices can cost stability, planetary output, pops/operational capacity, or research, and offer a durable understanding or scar transformation. Spreading symptoms never silently erase or replace the original feature.

**Completion:** resolve two local incidents and establish a containment or observation protocol. If the world becomes unstable, the mandatory fallback is evacuation/remote containment plus a costly project; not a forced reset. Earlier cautious study lowers incident severity; exploitation gives stronger research opportunities but makes later defense and chamber activation harder.

## Chapter 5 — The Network of Silence

The counter-pattern points to several actual star systems: one carries a delayed reflection, one is unnaturally quiet, and one contains an interrupted transmission. The network is a relationship among places, not a galaxy-wide abstract meter.

**Discovery scene — the silent system:** The first ship arrives expecting a weak echo and instead finds an apparently empty system whose navigation beacons, ambient radio, and natural particle noise all fall silent at the same boundary. The silence is not absence: instruments show a star's ordinary oscillation being cancelled in precisely timed notches. In the dark hemisphere of a barren moon, the team finds parallel grooves fused into the rock. Their spacing matches the Nullwardens' weapon harmonics. The grooves do not form writing or a recognizable machine; they are the surviving edges of something repeatedly damped. When the ship sends a low-power copy of the Phase I interval, the cancellation stops for one heartbeat. A faint return appears in the moon's subsurface and then vanishes. One interval in the return exactly matches the final fragment preserved in the Missing Expedition record. A rescued expedition scientist recognizes a tiny irregularity in the old carrier signal; if that scientist was lost, the same match comes from the archived telemetry and no voice or memory is invented. The crew records several Nullwarden-like cancellation passes in old regolith, older than the current expedition but with no identifiable maker or date. The system has been suppressing the same broken signal that took the expedition, though the evidence cannot show whether the Nullwardens caused the loss or were responding to it.

The player decides whether to **keep the system dark** (protect the site and gain the cleanest evidence, but leave the return uncharacterized), **repeat the pulse under shielding** (learn the return's structure at stability/resource risk), or **broadcast a counter-pattern** (obtain a stronger network bearing and tactical warning, while making the empire's presence detectable). A lost ship or inaccessible site yields a degraded archive/intercept version of the observation, not the full scene's result. Keeping dark produces a reliable suppression trace; repeating reveals that the silence is actively maintained; broadcasting reveals a bearing toward convergence and alerts the network. The evidence makes it plausible that the Nullwardens suppress something in the system. It establishes neither who made the grooves nor what the network is for.

**Other expeditions:** dispatch science ships to at least two distinct systems and complete projects at real sites. The player chooses a listening array, a mobile expedition, or a paired survey. Each discovers a clue and carries a cost: upkeep and exposure, ship time and possible loss, or reduced detail. A resonance lab may be built on an appropriate world to improve analysis, with stability and planetary side effects. The delayed reflection and interrupted transmission can be followed in either order; a missing, inaccessible or lost site has an archive or long-range observation substitute.

**Completion:** chart two usable nodes and identify a convergence location. Earlier Phase I choice affects one site's access or interpretation, never the only route. The silent system supplies the chapter's strongest emotional and scientific evidence: a pattern has been repeatedly silenced, and the Nullwardens may be responsible for the suppression. The network remains silent when not stimulated, suggesting a system in standby rather than an active empire; authorship and purpose remain unknown.

## Chapter 6 — The Wardens

A larger, adapted Nullwarden force arrives at the convergence approach. It has changed after observing the empire's tactics. Its objective is to disable the listening network and re-seal the scar, not occupy territory. The battle is a test of the empire's accumulated decisions and technology, not a mandatory fleet check.

**Play:** engage with a prepared fleet; use sensor deception and a trap; or defend a node while a science ship completes a countermeasure. Adaptive Resonance Armor rewards prior controlled exposure; Phased Sensors reveal approach behavior; counter-pattern analysis supports decoys and reduced engagement strength. A military-weak empire can survive through layered starbase defense, evacuation and a research countermeasure, paying in time/resources and possibly losing an outpost or node. Retreat is valid; recovered debris or an intact signal enables the chamber path with a cost.

**Completion:** break contact, drive off, or outlast the force and obtain evidence from wreckage, signals or observation. Loss changes Chapter 7's component requirements, not its availability. If the player refuses the conflict, evacuation and a sealed node provide a difficult but complete route.

## Chapter 7 — The Resonance Chamber

The convergence location contains a dormant structure around a stable boundary condition. The structure is not identified as a machine built by a known people. Activating it may let the empire shape the reply, but the chamber also amplifies every uncontrolled part of the scar.

**Play:** assemble a multi-stage operation using a staffed research site, a power/material commitment and at least two prior clues. Resonance Field Control becomes available for normal research during this preparation stage, before the activation choice, and can improve safeguards or reduce the activation cost. It is useful but optional: without it, the player may use more resources and research capacity to build a one-use containment array, accept a narrower observation-only operation, or take a higher-risk protocol. Choose the chamber's foundation: isolate the contamination site's signal (lower risk, limited reach); synchronize it with the chamber (high research, high instability); or redirect its pulse (military/strategic utility and risk of off-world effects). If the contamination world is inaccessible, use its surviving data or a recovered sample; the capital scar remains its own cost and is not substituted as the source. Each stage has a decision, explicit cost and player action; interruption retains completed preparation and offers a resume, downscale, or abandon route. Stability changes risk and available safeguards but never creates an unwinnable lock. Class-specific interfaces frame the operation as consent and safety for organics, collective partitioning for hives, or process isolation and identity integrity for machines.

**Completion:** complete the chosen activation or deliberately seal the chamber after a minimum viable observation. A catastrophic experiment is an outcome with a persistent scar, not a campaign softlock. Chapter 8 follows any route with appropriately limited evidence.

## Chapter 8 — The Countermark

The chamber answers with a countermark: a repeated pattern that appears in the scar, the Nullwardens' suppression signal, and the distant network. For the first time, the empire can make an informed choice about this local relationship. The record is incomplete: it cannot name the originating intelligence or explain the broader EPOCH mystery.

**Endings:**

1. **Keep the boundary:** suppress the chamber and move the contamination-site state to Controlled; keep the capital's conclusion scar separately intact. This reduces local incidents and adds a continuing monitoring cost, but yields the least ongoing research and no fleet benefit. Small residual resonance risk remains if monitoring lapses. Phase III begins with a reliable but incomplete suppression map.
2. **Listen under guard:** leave the chamber active at low power and retain the contamination site as Controlled-with-channel; the capital scar remains intact. Gain a lasting research advantage and better early warning, offset by recurring incidents, monitoring upkeep and a risk that a future signal strengthens. Phase III begins with the richest local measurements but a live unresolved channel.
3. **Turn the signal outward:** redirect the countermark away from the contamination site; keep the capital scar intact and mark the source site as redirected/Active rather than cured. The empire gains a durable defensive warning and a modest military readiness advantage, while losing some safe local productivity and accepting higher instability, occasional incidents and the risk that the network traces the empire. Phase III begins with an exposed outbound bearing and tactical warning, but the empire is already visible to whatever listens.
4. **Break the instrument:** collapse the chamber after recording the countermark. The contamination site (if accessible) gains a permanent fracture/ruin feature and reduced local output or research capacity; if inaccessible, that physical result is retained in the record and the recovered archive is damaged instead. The capital conclusion scar and its full Phase I economic penalty remain intact. The empire removes the chamber's ongoing military and incident exposure and gains no continuing research or fleet benefit, but residual resonance can surface as rare, weak echoes. Phase III begins with a damaged countermark fragment and the safest immediate posture, but the least precise map.

These are durable, mutually exclusive resolutions; none is a universal optimal outcome. Each preserves the permanent Phase I capital scar and its negative economic effect, records the contamination site's actual outcome, closes the situation explicitly, and leaves Phase III a different information and strategic starting position. If a Phase I expedition member survived and is available, an optional closing report may reflect that person's established expertise; if lost, an archive fragment or another researcher supplies the report. No dialogue restores a lost person or revises their recorded fate. The immediate danger is resolved and Phase II has a self-contained ending.

## Narrative voice and recurring evidence

The same corrupted interval and Phase I archive identifier recur in readings, wreckage and chamber output. Phase I personnel, if alive and available, may comment from their established knowledge, but no new character is presumed to survive an outcome where they were recorded lost. The Nullwardens remain mostly silent. Their behavior increasingly looks like quarantine, though the attacks and collateral damage remain real. The chamber countermark confirms a shared pattern only. It does not identify the Continuum, Architects, Universal Ascension Protocol, or final purpose.
