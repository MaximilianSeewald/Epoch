**Status:** binding content catalog. Technology names below are English design names; player-facing English and German copy is expected. None is granted free: campaign discoveries make a normal research option available at its story gate.

Chapter completion presents research availability, not a free tech grant. Exact costs/tier strength remain a balance review decision.

## Nullwardens

**First appearance (Chapter 2):** compact, angular ships with surfaces that seem to erase nearby starlight. Their emission is a broken cancellation waveform matching the scar's pulse. They attack the scar source and defensive assets that shield it, disengaging when their objective is denied or their force is damaged. They do not communicate intelligibly. The empire has no justified name or motive at this point; “Nullwardens” is a researcher shorthand that becomes the recorded designation after wreck analysis.

**First force:** a small screening group with one durable command hull, escorts and a support/interference craft. It is sized as a serious local defense crisis for a typical empire at the five-year post-conclusion point, with strength keyed to the empire's campaign development rather than an assumed fixed year. It can defeat an unprepared player, but is not designed as a surprise capital-killing doomstack. It focuses on the scar system and presents warning behavior before full engagement. Its capabilities disrupt sensors and favor coordinated volleys; it is vulnerable to concentrated fire, decoys and strong defenses.

**Alternatives:** engage with a prepared fleet; defend behind a fortified starbase and evacuate; conduct a science diversion while defense buys time; arrange available allied support; or withdraw and recover evidence. The latter routes impose real costs and possible loss. A weak science empire has at least one deliberate, demanding route based on preparation and research countermeasures; it never receives a free victory. Failure can destroy ships or a node and still opens a lower-yield clue path.

**Second force (Chapter 6):** larger and adapted to observed tactics, with escorts configured against the player's most-used defense profile and a signal-jamming element. It attacks the network node, gives the player a short tactical read, and can be countered using adaptive armor, phased sensors, wreck-derived decoys, a trap or layered defenses. It retreats or is repelled through combat, countermeasure, evacuation or deliberate withdrawal. The force remains a genuine naval threat with hulls, weapons, movement and the capacity to destroy assets; an event-only apparition is not an acceptable design outcome.

**Evidence:** recoverable debris identifies weapon harmonics and a damaged internal pattern. A captured transmission repeats a command-like interval. Neither evidence proves whether the Nullwardens are autonomous, directed, protective, or hostile by intent. Destroyed/lost wrecks have a poorer remote telemetry route.

## Planet Feature continuity matrix

| Phase I class / outcome | Phase II presentation | Must remain true |
|---|---|---|
| Organic scar | Physical ecological or settlement scar with the prior class-specific permanent effect visible in the appropriate feature category. | The original economic cost and severity remain; Resonance state adds presentation and later incident effects. |
| Hive scar | A scar in the shared environment, brood/coordination or sensory substrate. | The hive penalty persists as a real planetary consequence; it is not reframed as individual citizens' dreams. |
| Machine scar | Persistent fault geometry in memory, power, sensor or fabrication infrastructure. | The machine-specific loss persists; sentience category does not confer immunity. |
| Any scar worsened by Phase I neglect or outcome | More severe baseline feature/description and matching Phase II incident intensity. | Severity follows saved outcome, and later control does not erase historical cost. |

The Phase II state feature may be modified to show Latent, Active, Unstable or Controlled status. The underlying origin and Phase I cost remain recorded. A new affected planet can receive a secondary feature only through an explicit chapter event and consequential choice; planetary spread is not silent or automatic.
