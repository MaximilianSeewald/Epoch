# Phase II: Technologies, Nullwardens and Planet Features

**Status:** binding content catalog. Technology names below are English design names; player-facing English and German copy is expected. None is granted free: campaign discoveries make a normal research option available at its story gate.

Every technology becomes a normal research option at the exact campaign gate listed above; narrative discovery never grants it for free. The chain is acyclic: Spectroscopy → Adaptive Resonance Armor; Spectroscopy → Phased Sensors; Chapter 4 containment → Contamination-Neutral Infrastructure; then Adaptive Armor plus either Infrastructure or Phased Sensors → Field Control. Field Control is offered before chamber activation. If the player has not researched an offered technology when needed, the fallback costs more resources/research or limits the operation; the campaign does not wait forever or deadlock. Exact research costs and tier strength remain balance choices.

## Nullwardens

**First appearance (Chapter 2):** compact, angular ships with surfaces that seem to erase nearby starlight. Their emission is a broken cancellation waveform matching the scar's pulse. They attack the scar source and defensive assets that shield it, disengaging when their objective is denied or their force is damaged. They do not communicate intelligibly. The empire has no justified name or motive at this point; “Nullwardens” is a researcher shorthand that becomes the recorded designation after wreck analysis.

**First force:** a small screening group with one durable command hull, escorts and a support/interference craft. It is sized as a serious local defense crisis for a typical empire at the five-year post-conclusion point, with strength keyed to the empire's campaign development rather than an assumed fixed year. It can defeat an unprepared player, but is not designed as a surprise capital-killing doomstack. It focuses on the scar system and presents warning behavior before full engagement. Its capabilities disrupt sensors and favor coordinated volleys; it is vulnerable to concentrated fire, decoys and strong defenses.

**Alternatives:** engage with a prepared fleet; defend behind a fortified starbase and evacuate; conduct a science diversion while defense buys time; arrange available allied support; or withdraw and recover evidence. The latter routes impose real costs and possible loss. A weak science empire has at least one deliberate, demanding route based on preparation and research countermeasures; it never receives a free victory. Failure can destroy ships or a node and still opens a lower-yield clue path.

**Second force (Chapter 6):** larger and adapted to observed tactics, with escorts configured against the player's most-used defense profile and a signal-jamming element. It attacks the network node, gives the player a short tactical read, and can be countered using adaptive armor, phased sensors, wreck-derived decoys, a trap or layered defenses. It retreats or is repelled through combat, countermeasure, evacuation or deliberate withdrawal. The force remains a genuine naval threat with hulls, weapons, movement and the capacity to destroy assets; an event-only apparition is not an acceptable design outcome.

**Evidence:** recoverable debris identifies weapon harmonics and a damaged internal pattern. A captured transmission repeats a command-like interval. Neither evidence proves whether the Nullwardens are autonomous, directed, protective, or hostile by intent. Destroyed/lost wrecks have a poorer remote telemetry route.

## Planet Feature continuity matrix

| Phase I record | Phase II presentation | Must remain true |
|---|---|---|
| Original contamination site (any class) | Planet Feature records the actual world and Phase I result: contained, worsened, spread or otherwise resolved. Organic expression is ecological/settlement damage; hive expression is shared environment/brood/coordination; machine expression is memory, power, sensor or fabrication fault. | Preserve its existing effect and saved severity. Do not imply this is the capital scar unless locations match. If lost/inaccessible, the recorded outcome remains and Phase II uses archive/sample/remote evidence. |
| Capital conclusion scar (organic, hive, or machine) | Separate permanent Planet Feature on the Phase I capital, expressing its established class-specific scar and economic penalty. | Preserve the exact negative gameplay consequence. It remains even if the contamination site is somewhere else or no longer accessible. |
| Both records on the same world | One planet may display a unified feature presentation with clearly retained contamination-site and conclusion-scar aspects. | Combine presentation only; neither recorded outcome nor effect is merged away. |
| Either world lost, transferred or inaccessible | Preserve its historical feature/result in the empire's campaign record; use surviving records, samples or an explicitly established accessible study site for new play. | Do not assume current ownership or silently move the feature/economic penalty to another world. |
| Any contamination site worsened by Phase I neglect/outcome | More severe contamination-site feature/description and matching Phase II incident intensity. | Severity follows saved outcome; later control does not erase the historical cost. |

The contamination-site feature may be modified to show Latent, Active, Unstable or Controlled status. The capital conclusion-scar feature remains distinct and permanent unless both records occupy the same world, in which case both aspects remain visible. A new affected planet can receive a secondary feature only through an explicit chapter event and consequential choice; planetary spread is not silent or automatic.
